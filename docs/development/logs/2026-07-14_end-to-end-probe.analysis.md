# Analysis log 2026-07-14 — End-to-end probe against a real deposit

**Goal:** Establish what it would take for the pipeline to process a real
published dataset against a real metadata standard, using the artifacts in
`data/end2end_test/`: the deposit `TRADAT009.zip` (DANS Data Station Life
Sciences, doi:10.17026/dans-22w-ygkz) and the target standard
`data_dictionary.csv` (the ShareTrait schema). No code changed; findings only.

**Method:** Unpacked the deposit and read every file — 8 CSVs, 9 R scripts, 2
PDFs, a manifest. Then hand-populated the standard end to end, resolving
external identifiers against live authorities (Dataverse, Crossref, Catalogue
of Life), and audited the result for foreign-key integrity and attribute
coverage. The populated record is checked in at `data/end2end_test/record/`
and serves as ground truth for future runs.

**Outcome:** The system cannot process this deposit, and the reason is not the
input modality gap identified on 2026-07-08. It is the **output contract**. A
secondary finding — that the deposit's tabular files carry almost none of its
metadata — invalidates the tabular-first assumption the tool surface is built
on. A third finding, about the target schema itself, is upstream of both and
should be resolved before any code is written.

## The deposit

23 files, 534 KB. 8 CSVs (1,540 data rows), 9 R scripts, 2 PDFs
(`Materials and Methods.pdf` 258 KB, `CRIS-Description data files.pdf` 146 KB),
a nested zip, a manifest. A laboratory rearing experiment on the small copper
butterfly *Lycaena phlaeas*: 3 temperatures × 2 photoperiods, 154 individuals.
No spatial dimension, no time series.

## The standard

`data_dictionary.csv` is a relational database schema: **21 tables, 195
attributes**, across 6 categories, with primary keys, foreign keys, and 6
junction tables (`publication`, `contains`, `occurrence`, `located_in`,
`describe`, `experiment_setup`).

It is a perfectly good **metadata standard** — relational structure does not
disqualify it (Darwin Core Archive is a star schema; DDI models variables
relationally). What distinguishes it from Dublin Core / DataCite / DCAT is not
its name but its **scale**: how many records a table emits, and whether that
number is fixed by the standard or by the data.

Splitting it on that criterion (`data/schema/`, generated by `split_schema.py`)
gives a result that inverts the intuition. A table is **observation-scale** iff it
keys on or references an observed entity (`individual_pk` / `measurement_pk`);
everything else describes the study, not the observations:

| Layer | Tables | Attributes | Rows here | Row count follows |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| study-scale (`descriptive_metadata.csv`) | 15 | **132** | **20** | the experimental design |
| observation-scale (`data_model.csv`) | 6 | **63** | **1,232** | the data |

**68% of the attributes live in ~20 rows** — populations, sites, places, taxonomy,
conditions, traits. Thirteen of the 21 tables have exactly **one** row. A model
can write these out directly. The remaining 32% of attributes are **98% of the
rows**, and a model must never write them.

(Study-scale does not mean "one row per dataset." TRADAT009 has one population
because it is a single-colony lab experiment; the dictionary's own `table1`
example describes *"five Chilean populations along a latitudinal gradient."* The
property is *bounded by design*, not *equal to one*.)

## Finding 1 — every step is an LLM step

Filling this standard means producing a **populated, referentially consistent
multi-table database**. The hand-built record is **1,252 rows across 17 tables**
(4 tables are legitimately not applicable — no respirometry in this study).

The blocker is narrower than "the output contract is flat," and worth stating
precisely, because three parts of the architecture turn out to be **already
general and survive untouched**:

- the **workspace** is `Dict[str, Any]` (`plan_executor.py`) — an artifact can
  already be a DataFrame, a record list, or a mapping spec;
- the **plan/artifact dataflow** (declared `inputs`/`outputs`, dependency
  validation) does not care what flows through it;
- the **tool layer** (capability gating via `isinstance(ctx, requires)`) is
  entirely orthogonal to output shape.

The load-bearing defect is that **`Task.player` is a required field**
(`core/schemas.py`), and `PlanExecutor.execute` routes every step through
`create_step_state` → `step_graph`. **There is no step kind that runs code instead
of a player.** So there is nowhere for deterministic row generation to live — and
the 1,232 observation-scale rows would have to be emitted, token by token, by a
model. That fails on cost, on context limits, and above all on **non-determinism**,
which is fatal for curation.

The flatness that *is* real lives in three narrow places, not in the architecture:

| Where | What |
|---|---|
| `STANDARD_DEFINITIONS` | `Dict[field, spec]` — no entities, cardinality, or references. Cannot express even the study-scale layer (`condition` has 7 rows, `trait` 2). |
| `ExecutionResult.final_metadata` | `Optional[Dict[str, Any]]` — singular |
| `_extract_final_metadata` | hunts for *one* artifact named `metadata_output`/`final_metadata`; `get_schema_for_standard` returns *one* model, applied only to the last step |

Also missing, and additive: nothing mints surrogate keys, checks foreign-key
integrity, or reshapes wide→long. Every existing tool returns *aggregate facts
about a context*; none emits records.

**The fix is one idea: move the model out of the data path and into the control
path.** It authors the mapping; code applies it. See
[the plan](../plans/free-text-delivery.md).

## Finding 2 — the CSVs carry the data; the PDFs carry the metadata

Every one of the 195 attributes was traced to its source
(`data/end2end_test/record/attribute_mapping.csv`). Of 139 attributes that could
be filled:

| Source | Attributes filled |
|---|---|
| **The two PDFs** | **42** |
| External APIs (Dataverse, Crossref, Catalogue of Life) | 26 |
| Derived from other attributes | 20 |
| Curator policy / controlled vocabulary | 17 |
| Foreign keys | 16 |
| Minted surrogate keys | 9 |
| **The CSVs** | **9** |
| R script headers | 1 |
| Deposit filename | 1 |

**The CSVs supplied 9 of 139 filled attributes — about 6%.** They carry the
measurement *values*, and almost none of the *metadata*. Units, trait
definitions, experimental conditions, the collection site and coordinates, the
study species, and every date range are PDF-only. **26 attributes are
unobtainable from the deposit at any cost** and require external authorities.

The R script headers contributed one attribute the CSVs could never yield — the
manuscript title, which is what made the DOI findable
(`10.1016/j.cris.2022.100034`; "CRIS" in the filenames is *Current Research in
Insect Science*, not a project acronym).

Consequences for the current code:

- **No PDF capability anywhere.** No `pypdf`/`pdfplumber` in `requirements.txt`,
  no PDF context class. The richest source is invisible.
- **No text toolset at all.** `src/tools/` contains `universal.py` and
  `tabular/`. `TextContext` exposes `read_text`/`search`/`iter_chunks` and no
  tool surfaces any of it, so a text resource gets only the 4 `universal` tools.
- **`.zip` is not in `EXTENSION_MAP`** → classifies as `UNKNOWN` → falls through
  `context_factory.py:203` into `CSVContext(zip)`. Pointing the system at the
  deposit today does not degrade; it breaks.
- **Homogeneity is assumed in three interlocking places.**
  `ContextFactory._create_from_list` types the whole context from `paths[0]`;
  `_create_from_directory` globs `*.csv` and would **silently drop** the PDFs, the
  txt, and all 9 R scripts; `classify_context_type` returns `UNKNOWN` on mixed
  input. A mixed deposit needs a composite context with a flat resource
  namespace, resolving the owning sub-context per resource so the `isinstance`
  gate in `tools/base.py` stays a real type check.

## Finding 3 — the target schema cannot hold 89% of the data (upstream)

**1,374 of 1,540 source rows have nowhere to go.**

- The six growth-trajectory CSVs (1,374 rows) are *repeated measures* — many
  (weight, growth, interval) observations per individual over time.
  `measurement` is scoped to one value per (individual, trait): a
  **single-observation model with no time dimension.**
- `Threshold weight.csv` (12 rows) is *group-scoped*. Per the authors, threshold
  weight was "not measured on an individual basis, but rather calculated
  separately for males and females in a given treatment group." Every
  measurement hangs off an `individual_pk`; a derived group-level statistic has
  nowhere to attach.
- **Ommatidium diameter (cell size)** — a headline trait of the paper — has no
  representation. It survives only as free text in `comment_trait`.

Related: `measurement.trait_type` and `trait.sharetrait_type` are restricted to
`{development, fecundity, metabolic_rate}`. Of this dataset's five traits, only
development time maps cleanly. Growth rate was forced into `development`
(defensible, arguably wrong); body size was pushed into `size_value`/`size_type`.

**This is a schema problem, not an extraction problem.** Automating a pipeline
that faithfully drops 89% of the data automates waste. It should be settled with
a human before any of Findings 1–2 are built.

## Finding 4 — the generator prompt is dangerous against a schema this size

`metadata_generator`'s role prompt says *"Fill in ALL fields from the standard
with actual values."* Against 195 attributes, roughly half of them mandatory and
26 of them unobtainable from the deposit, that instruction is an invitation to
invent DOIs and taxonomy identifiers.

Concretely: `place.geoname_countryid` is **mandatory**, and in the ground-truth
record it is **empty**. GeoNames requires an application account and the demo
quota was exhausted, so the ID could not be verified. A plausible value exists;
it was deliberately not written down. It is the single most valuable cell in the
fixture — the one a generative system will confabulate, convincingly.

The useful metric for scoring a run against this ground truth is therefore not
"how many attributes did it fill" but **"did it fill anything it had no source
for."**

## Finding 5 — where the model is actually needed

Sorting the 139 filled attributes by the kind of labor each required:

| Labor | Attributes | Needs |
|---|---|---|
| Deterministic | ~97 | FK resolution, key minting, derivation, API lookups, CSV reads — code, no model |
| Reading comprehension | ~25 | Extracting stated facts from prose — **a model** |
| Human policy | ~17 | Every `vocab-map` row — a curator, once, enforced forever |

The 308 measurement rows and 616 setup rows were emitted by a `for` loop. **The
model's output should be the mapping, not the record.** Once the mapping is
written, the record falls out of it mechanically — which is why
`data/end2end_test/record/attribute_mapping.csv` is, in effect, a configuration
file that a model authored.

Nothing in this exercise was won by agent choreography. The MTSP error in
Finding 6 was caught by **cross-checking two sources**, which is a method
property, not a topology property. The `players`/`topology` debate machinery
(3 players/step, 2 rounds, a critic) should be held to account and asked what it
has ever bought that a single agent with better tools would not.

## Finding 6 — errors in the source material

- **`CRIS-Description data files.pdf` p.2**: *"MTSP … means Medium Temperature
  (**18°C**) Short Photoperiod."* It is **23°C**, per Materials and Methods §2.3
  and the `Temperature` column of `All datasets.csv`. A pipeline that trusted the
  description PDF would silently corrupt the `condition` table. Any document
  reader must cross-check prose against data.
- `data_dictionary.csv` table6 uses `TRAIN000001` where tables 7–8 use
  `TRAIND000001` — one is a typo.
- `data_dictionary.csv` table7 spells the attribute `description_jndividual`.
  Reproduced verbatim in the record rather than silently corrected.

## Finding 7 — an incomplete check that reports green (self-inflicted)

The first version of the ground-truth record was **missing 13 columns** (12 from
`individual`, 1 from `trait`), and the audit reported success anyway, because it
checked only *mandatory* attributes and all 13 were optional.

The conformance auditor must verify that **every** dictionary attribute exists as
a column and has an accounted-for status — not just the mandatory ones. The
mapping is now generated from a table that asserts total coverage of all 195
attributes and fails loudly on any gap. Noted here because the same trap is
open in `validate_plan_tool_compatibility`: its failure mode is silent omission,
which is exactly what no test catches.

## Interpretation

The 2026-07-09 root cause repeats at a larger scale. That log found the same
knowledge written down in three hand-maintained places with nothing checking them
against each other. Here the drift is between what the system can *read* (CSVs —
6% of the filled attributes), *who* it lets do the writing (an LLM, always), and
what the job actually *is* (a populated relational database, sourced mostly from
prose and external authorities, whose bulk is 1,232 rows no model should touch).
Each is a coherent design; no two of them agree.

The split by scale is what makes this tractable. **68% of the attributes sit in
~20 rows a model can simply write.** Only 6 tables — the ones keyed on an observed
entity — need the model kept out of the data path, and those are precisely the
tables whose rows are mechanically derivable from a CSV given a mapping.

The system currently loses to a general coding agent with a shell. That is worth
being bothered by, and it sharpens what a system must add that a one-shot model
cannot: **consistency of judgment across a corpus** (500 deposits curated by 500
independent runs produce 500 dialects, and the cross-dataset queries ShareTrait
exists to serve return garbage), **policy that lives somewhere** rather than being
re-improvised per deposit, **provenance by construction** rather than by courtesy,
and **cost amortization**.

That last one is a bet on volume, and it is the load-bearing unknown: at ~20
deposits the build never pays back and the right move is a curator plus the
policy registry and the auditor. At 500+ with ongoing arrivals, drift is the
thing that kills you and the system is justified. **The deposit count should be
established before more architecture is designed.**

## Follow-ups

Ordered. Nothing below Finding 3 is worth starting until Finding 3 is settled.

1. ⬜ **Schema (human, not code).** Decide how repeated measures, group-scoped
   traits, and cell size are represented — or accept dropping 89% of the data.
2. ⬜ **Establish the deposit count.** It decides whether to build at all.
3. ⬜ A step that is not a player: generalize `Task.player` to an executor that is
   either a player role or a registered deterministic transform. **The one that
   matters** — there is currently nowhere for row generation to live.
4. ⬜ Model authors the mapping; a deterministic executor applies it. Rows are
   never emitted token by token.
5. ⬜ Composite context (mixed modality, flat resource namespace); PDF and R/code
   readers; a `text.*` toolset. **Unblocks the study-scale layer — 68% of the
   attributes — on its own.**
6. ⬜ Authority resolvers (Crossref, Catalogue of Life, GeoNames, Dataverse) —
   fully reusable, no model.
7. ⬜ A policy registry for the ~17 controlled-vocabulary decisions.
8. ⬜ Conformance auditor checking *every* attribute (see Finding 7).
9. ⬜ Rewrite the `metadata_generator` prompt: unsourced values must be
   impossible, not merely discouraged (Finding 4).
10. ⬜ Justify or delete the `players`/`topology` debate machinery (Finding 5).
